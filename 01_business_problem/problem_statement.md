# Business Problem

When a team underperforms, replacing its leader is the default response —
in football, sacking the manager; in an organization, replacing a
struggling department head or team lead. But teams in a slump also tend
to recover on their own, given time. Without a matched comparison, a
recovery that would have happened anyway gets misattributed to the
leadership change.

This project tests that attribution problem directly, using 14 seasons of
real Bundesliga match data (2012/13–2025/26) as a clean, decision-relevant
proxy: a managerial sacking is a leadership-change decision made under
real time pressure, with a measurable before/after outcome (points per
game), and — critically — a large enough pool of comparable teams that
did *not* change managers to build a genuine control group.

**The question, in a people-analytics frame:** when performance improves
after a leadership change, how much of that improvement is the change
itself, versus teams simply recovering from their worst point? The
method used here — matched comparison against a control group, not a
bare before/after — is the same discipline that should sit behind any
claim that a reorg, a manager swap, or a leadership change caused a
turnaround, rather than merely preceding one.
